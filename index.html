The portfolio

Four products, one driver

The same person is, at different moments, a Yango moto-taxi driver earning Upahar points, an Express courier between ride-hailing shifts, a dot on the GPS dashboard's map, and, if this launches, someone paying down a motorcycle.

Live, finehusig-ai/yapigo

Drivra Upahar

Driver rewards. FastAPI + Supabase. 1,793 drivers on the roster, 261 active in the last 30 days (14.6%). Retention, not acquisition, is the constraint. The most tested, most production-hardened piece of the stack.

Live, needs rethinkinghusig-ai/drivra_express_backend

Drivra Express

B2B delivery. Own Postgres, own gamification system built independently of Upahar's. Early-stage: 22 commits, no test suite, no managed backups, and a critical CORS gap (below). Needs partner APIs and point-to-point delivery before it can grow past merchant/hub orders.

Live, needs rethinkingDrivra-Mobility/gps

GPS Dashboard

Fleet telematics: live map, idle detection, and a genuinely useful misuse-detection feature. No backend of its own: it reads and writes straight into Upahar's Supabase tables with a public key, protected only by row-level security. That pattern is the single biggest platform risk on this page.

Not builtproposed, standalone

Rent-to-Own Financing

Let a driver finance their motorcycle through Drivra, using their Upahar activity history as the credit signal instead of a bank file. The product this whole document was triggered by. Full design on Rent-to-Own.

The headline finding

A fintech product can't be built on top of today's data pattern without fixing it first

Two of the four systems above already share one database in a way that's caused a real production incident and a documented security gap. A new product holding KYC documents and loan ledgers should not sit inside, or next to, that pattern.

What actually happened: Upahar's own Supabase tables, including admin logins and OTP codes, had zero row-level security until a recent migration closed it. GPS Dashboard still reads and writes those tables directly from the browser with a public anon key, RLS is its only access control. That same shared project already hit its free-tier egress quota once, traced to GPS Dashboard over-polling. Full detail: Architecture.

Debt inventory

What's wrong, ranked by how much it matters

Critical, fix regardless of anything else.
  • Express's CORS policy is wide open: allow_origins=["*"] plus allow_credentials=True. Any website can make authenticated requests against Express today.
  • GPS Dashboard has unrestricted, direct, client-side access to Upahar's production tables. A public anon key plus RLS is the entire access-control boundary.
High, already causing real problems.
  • Supabase egress quota was exceeded once already, traced to GPS Dashboard over-polling.
  • PostgREST's 1,000-row response cap silently truncates GPS Dashboard's wide-date-range queries: a correctness bug, not just a performance one.
  • Express has no managed backup story: Postgres on a container volume, no confirmed automated backup.
  • Express has zero test files anywhere in the repo.
Moderate issues, and the full fix plan
  • Express runs two competing schema sources on every startup (create_all() and Alembic), which can silently diverge.
  • Upahar and Express each independently built their own points/badges/quests system: duplicated effort, not broken.
  • No canonical driver identity across products: two databases, two drivers tables, no link.

Full ranked list with the specific low-lift fixes and the larger investments: Architecture.

The vision

One canonical driver identity, not one shared database

The fix is not a bigger shared database: Upahar and GPS Dashboard already sharing one is the worst-behaved part of the stack. The fix is narrower and smaller than that.

1Each product keeps owning its own operational data
2One canonical driver identity, read by every product, owned by none
3A narrow, versioned API between products, never direct table access
4A small batch-fed reporting database for cross-product signal

This is deliberately right-sized: a small identity service behind a plain API, no message bus; one small Postgres reporting database, no warehouse product. That's confirmed against what teams at this scale actually adopt, not an enterprise default. Full reasoning and the "explicitly not needed yet" list: Architecture.

The new product

Rent-to-own motorcycle financing

A local competitor, RYD Nepal, already runs this model with no app and no credit check. Drivra's edge: this can live inside an app drivers already use daily, where real earnings and tenure history, not a bank statement, drive approval and terms.

Regulation decides the shape

Only a licensed lender may originate consumer loans in Nepal (BAFIA §31). Recommended path: partner with a licensed hire-purchase company (Path A), Drivra as the distribution and underwriting-data layer. Avoids a NPR 300M capital bar; still needs counsel to confirm repossession rights for Drivra's specific structure.

System design

Standalone service, own repo, own Postgres, own Supabase project (never shared with Upahar/GPS). Reuses Upahar's proven FastAPI + SQLAlchemy + Alembic stack. DIY Textract + Rekognition eKYC at roughly $0.07/verification, about 30 to 100 times cheaper than a global vendor.

Credit scoring v1

Rules-based scorecard, not ML: tenure, earnings consistency, streaks, tier, all already in Upahar. Cold-start applicants get a guarantor or higher down payment instead of a decline. No telematics/remote-disable in v1: reputational risk outweighs the benefit this early.

Full competitive research, the regulatory citations, the data model, and the payments design: Rent-to-Own.

Method, and what's a fact versus an estimate

This isn't a speculative strategy memo. The product-portfolio and architecture sections come from directly reading the five relevant repositories (yapigo, drivra_express_backend, gps_dashboard, drivra, drivra-web): their models, migrations, deployment configs, and their own READMEs. The rent-to-own design draws on that same codebase reading plus external research: competitor products (RYD Nepal, M-KOPA, Tugende, Watu, Moove), Nepal's lending and KYC regulation (NRB/BAFIA), Nepal's payment rail landscape (eSewa, Khalti, Fonepay, ConnectIPS), and cost/infra benchmarking. Where a number is a genuine estimate rather than a confirmed fact, it's labeled as one.
