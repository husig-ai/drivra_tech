Competitive landscape

A local competitor is already running this exact model

RYD Nepal (rydnepal.com) rents Hero Super Splendor 125cc motorcycles to Kathmandu gig drivers today. Founded 2025, self-funded, 120+ bikes, claims 500+ active riders, single-SKU fleet.

Only one of RYD's three plans actually leads to ownership — worth noting since most of their marketing describes "own the bike in 18 months" as if it applies broadly. KYC is driving license + citizenship copy only, processed in under 30 minutes, in person or via WhatsApp — no income proof, no credit check, no guarantor, no app (web form + WhatsApp + in-person at their Kapan workshop). Zero down payment. Free service every 1,500km, 24/7 breakdown line, a replacement bike within 30–40 minutes if repair runs long, and a welcome "Sagoon kit" (helmet, phone mount, raincoat) plus a Rs 1,500 fuel coupon on pickup. Missed-payment mechanics are not disclosed anywhere public — marketed only as "no penalty fees, no credit-bureau drama." Their sharpest marketing copy targets other unnamed rental competitors ("bikes deteriorate by month two," "you fix it, I'll think about it"), not banks or predatory lenders. Trust signal: only three self-hosted testimonials, zero independent reviews found anywhere (no Trustpilot, Google reviews, Reddit) — their credibility is entirely self-curated, which is itself an opening.

Category-wide finding

None of these companies — RYD Nepal included — have any real, independently documented UX: no screenshots, no coverage on UX pattern libraries (Mobbin, Page Flows, Dribbble), for any of them. A well-designed, actually-screenshotted, transparent driver experience would be a genuine differentiator in this category on its own, separate from pricing or terms.

Recommended positioning

  • Lead differentiator: the only rent-to-own offer built inside an app the driver already uses daily for rewards/earnings tracking — real usage history translates directly into faster approval and better terms for loyal drivers, which neither RYD Nepal (web/WhatsApp-only) nor a bank (needs a traditional file) can offer.
  • A compliant no-debt-spiral policy, not just messaging. M-KOPA's explicit "missed payment extends the term, no penalty" policy is the strongest trust lever found in this research — and it happens to already be achievable as a compliant policy in Nepal, not just an aspirational one: NRB's hire-purchase rules cap penalty interest at 2pp/year and explicitly ban penalty-on-penalty compounding (see Regulation, below). Say this explicitly in marketing, backed by the actual contract terms.
  • A visible ownership-progress tracker — percentage-owned, remaining installments, clear payoff date. No competitor researched currently shows this.
  • Tier-linked terms. Better down payment or priority allocation for higher-tier Upahar drivers — a synergy this product structurally has and RYD Nepal cannot replicate.

Nepal regulation

The single biggest thing shaping this design

Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) regulation, not engineering choice, determines what's actually buildable:

  • Under BAFIA, only a licensed BFI (Class A/B/C/D) may originate/hold consumer loans — a pure tech company cannot lend directly.
  • Vehicle financing specifically sits under a Hire Purchase Company license: NPR 300M minimum paid-up capital, a 60% LTV cap (≥40% down payment required), service fee capped at 1%, overdue-penalty interest capped at 2pp/year with no compounding, single-borrower concentration limits, an NRB "fit and proper" test for founders/directors.
  • CIB Nepal (the credit bureau) is only accessible to NRB-registered BFIs.
  • A licensed BFI gets BAFIA §57 self-help repossession — 35-day notice, no court order needed, three public-auction attempts before Debt Recovery Tribunal for any residual. An unlicensed lessor most likely only has ordinary contract/property remedies.
  • AML/KYC obligations apply regardless of structure: e-KYC with biometric verification under the Asset (Money) Laundering Prevention Act 2008; suspicious-transaction reports filed via goAML within 3 days; records retained a minimum of 5 years.

Recommendation for design purposes: architect for Path A

Closest fit to the "standalone service, internal credit signal, independent-of-Yango collection" decisions already made, and the cleanest regulatory footing without a NPR 300M capital commitment. This is a legal decision for Nepali financial-regulatory counsel, not resolved here — the data model treats "lender of record" as a field, so it doesn't block starting the KYC/underwriting/servicing build, but must be resolved before signing real contracts with drivers.

Driver journey

End to end

Discovery

An "Own Your Bike" entry point inside the existing Upahar app, surfaced by tenure/tier; a lightweight eligibility pre-check against existing performance data before any paperwork.

Application

Desired plan/vehicle, down payment amount, basic details.

KYC

Citizenship certificate + National ID + driving license photo capture, selfie for liveness/face-match, optional guarantor capture for thin-file applicants.

Underwriting decision

Rules-based scorecard → approve / decline / refer-to-manual, always admin-overridable with an audit-logged reason.

Contract & terms

Vehicle allocation, down payment, installment schedule, agreement type per the resolved legal path; e-signature/acceptance captured.

Disbursement/handover

Lien recorded (bluebook annotation + STRO filing where required), physical handover logged, first schedule activated.

Servicing

Recurring installments, reminders, payment intake, ledger posting, an always-visible ownership-progress tracker.

Ongoing monitoring

Periodic re-pull of Upahar (and, later, Express) performance data catches early drift before a payment is even missed.

Completion or default

Final installment → ownership transfer, lien released. Or: delinquency → dunning → default/repossession per the resolved legal path.

System architecture

A standalone service, plugged into the identity layer

Standalone service: own repo, own Postgres database, reusing Upahar's proven stack — FastAPI + SQLAlchemy 2.0 + Alembic + Jinja2/HTMX admin console — but its own deployment and security perimeter, since it holds KYC documents and financial data. It reads driver identity and performance data from the canonical identity layer described in Architecture — never direct database access into Upahar or Express.

Auth

Reuse Upahar's proven shape, decoupled: driver auth = phone + SparrowSMS OTP, issuing a JWT scoped to a distinct financing_driver audience — deliberately not SSO-trusting Upahar's session for v1, to keep security boundaries independent (a "Continue with Upahar" bridge is a reasonable later enhancement). Admin auth mirrors the bcrypt+JWT pattern, with more granular roles than Upahar's owner/staff split: KYC reviewer, underwriter, collections/ops, owner/compliance.

Storage: Supabase Storage over raw AWS S3 for v1

At this scale (a few thousand drivers × ~5 documents), storage/egress cost is trivial on either (low single-digit dollars/month). The deciding factor is integration effort: Supabase Storage is wired directly into Postgres Row-Level Security, so "only the assigned KYC reviewer or the document's owner can read this file" is a SQL policy against the same schema already modeling drivers/loans/officers — versus hand-rolling IAM/Cognito-equivalent logic for raw S3. Supabase Storage is itself S3-compatible under the hood, so this isn't lock-in; raw S3 becomes worth revisiting mainly for KMS-based per-key audit trails if a regulator later requires that level of detail. This must be its own Supabase project — not the one Upahar and GPS Dashboard already share.

eKYC: DIY AWS Textract + Rekognition for v1

Per-driver verification cost is roughly $0.07 (Textract AnalyzeID for the 2 ID documents + Rekognition liveness check + face-compare) versus $2–7 per verification for global vendors like Jumio/Onfido. Two Nepal-aware vendors exist (Shufti Pro, FACEKI) but neither publishes pricing — worth getting quotes, but DIY is roughly an order of magnitude cheaper and keeps v1 lean; the trade-off is owning fraud-detection tuning and liability in-house, acceptable for a manually-reviewed v1 where a human still signs off on every KYC approval regardless.

UI/UX

Three surfaces

Driver-facing

Lightweight web portal (not a second Flutter app), entry point linked from Upahar. Eligibility screen → application → KYC capture flow → guarantor entry (conditional) → status tracker → contract e-signature → ownership dashboard (running balance, % owned, next payment, one-tap pay) → reminders.

Admin console

New tabs on the existing "page + endpoint" convention: underwriting queue → KYC review → applications/contracts → ledger/collections dashboard → delinquency queue → vehicle/asset registry → NRB-facing reports (LTV, concentration, rate-cap compliance).

Field agent tool

A simple mobile-friendly page to record a cash payment collected in person — matches how Nepali MFIs and Hulas Finserv actually collect from lower-smartphone-penetration drivers.

Credit scoring v1

Internal-only, rules-based

Every thin-file lender researched (Watu, Tugende) starts here — a rules-based scorecard, not an ML model, since there's no proprietary repayment history yet to train on.

Inputs available day one, all from Upahar: tenure, lifetime trips/gross and their consistency, current/longest streak, active status, tier/XP. Once the identity layer exists, also from Express: delivery tenure and reliability, for drivers who do both.

Cold-start handling: guarantor requirement and/or higher down payment for drivers with little history — mirrors Watu's guarantor model and Tugende's reference-plus-training approach, rather than an outright decline.

Decision flow: hard gates (KYC complete, active status, minimum tenure) → weighted score → approve / decline / refer-to-manual-review, every decision and override audit-logged. Evolves into a behavioral model once the service has its own repayment-history dataset.

Explicitly not in v1

Telematics/GPS-based scoring or remote asset-disable enforcement — the Moove/M-KOPA/Watu pattern is powerful but adds IoT dependency and real reputational risk (documented tracker-collusion allegations against comparable East African lenders). Drivra's GPS Dashboard infrastructure already exists and could support this later — see Product — but it's a phase-3 decision, not a v1 one.

Payments & collections

No wallet in Nepal supports auto-debit — plan around that

Confirmed: Nepali wallets (eSewa, Khalti, Fonepay) do not support auto-debit/recurring pull — collection is a manual push each cycle. ConnectIPS/NCHL supports mandate-based direct debit, already used for hire-purchase EMI collection — the best path to real automation for drivers with a bank account.

Fee comparison: ConnectIPS is flat and cheap (Rs 2–8/transaction regardless of amount, NRB bars passing this to customers); Fonepay QR is free for merchants; eSewa/Khalti card-based loading runs 1.75–5%. Default drivers toward ConnectIPS or Fonepay QR; support eSewa/Khalti as fallback, not the primary path.

v1 architecture, matching how the market leader (Hulas Finserv) already operates: SMS reminder (reused SparrowSMS client) → driver pays via ConnectIPS/Fonepay QR (or eSewa/Khalti fallback) → reconciliation marks the ledger paid. ConnectIPS mandate as an opt-in automated path once manual flow is proven. Cash/agent collection fallback via the field agent tool. Delinquency bucketing (current → watch → substandard → loss) drives dunning and, eventually, the repossession trigger.

Compliance & risk

  • Every new table follows the Supabase PostgREST-grant-revoke migration convention.
  • KYC documents are the most sensitive data this system holds: Supabase Storage + RLS, encrypted at rest, 5-year minimum retention per AML rules, suspicious-activity reports via goAML within 3 days.
  • Vehicle lien registered both on the bluebook (everyday check) and at the Secured Transactions Registry Office under the Secured Transactions Act 2063 (formal legal priority) — confirm the interplay of the two with counsel.
  • If Path A: CIB Nepal checks and §57 repossession privileges flow through the partner — the partnership contract needs to define who owns which compliance obligation.
  • Any future IoT/GPS/remote-disable capability needs a tamper-evident, auditable data trail from day one, given documented disputes in comparable markets over tracker reliability.
